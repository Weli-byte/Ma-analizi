# ADR 0071 - League-model programme, Sprint 4c: separately trained per-league models, hierarchical model with learned variance components, nested walk-forward selection, extended features, exact-score audit

Date: 2026-10-23. Status: accepted. Builds on ADR 0063 to 0070. Nothing committed by Sprints 1 to 6 changes: the feature contract `lm-s4-features-v1`, the pre-registration, the licence manifest, the holdout files and every earlier output stay as they are.

## Context
The Sprint 6 report listed what the plan asked for and the programme did not build: (6) a full hierarchical team-strength model, (7) opponent-adjusted form and 90 / 365-day windows, (8) nested walk-forward for every choice (tuning had used one inner season), (9) exact-score scoring and a re-check of the "always over 68.8%" claim, (10) expected goals in the prediction record. The owner also asked, from the start, for one model trained separately for each league.

## Decisions
1. **Nested walk-forward** (`nested.py`). Every choice is scored on up to three inner folds: each of the last three training seasons predicted from the seasons strictly before it, with the result-lag embargo. With a single training season (first outer fold) the season is cut in time (earliest 70% fit, the rest validates, unpublished fit rows purged). The choice is made per target (1X2, over/under, BTTS), ties go to the earlier grid entry, every candidate and every inner fold is recorded, a candidate that does not train is recorded and never chosen. The boosted-tree tuner (`tune_nested`) scores each Optuna trial as the mean over the inner folds.
2. **Separately trained per-league models** (`per_league.PerLeagueDC`, `s4c_benchmark` for the boosted trees). One ridge Poisson / Dixon-Coles model per competition, fitted on that competition's matches only, penalty and time decay chosen for that competition and target by the nested selection; a competition under 500 training matches is LOCAL_INELIGIBLE and keeps the reference, visibly. One LightGBM per competition and target on that competition's matches only (Sprint 4 + extension features), nested-tuned, same 500-match rule.
3. **Hierarchical model with learned variance components** (`per_league.HierarchicalDC`). Team < country < world random effects plus competition terms (the Sprint 3 bridge structure), with the variance components (1 / penalty) selected per target by the nested walk-forward over the whole universe instead of being fixed numbers. This is empirical Bayes by predictive cross-validation, NOT a marginal-likelihood estimate; there is no time-varying team effect other than the decay.
4. **Extension features** (`extra_features.py`, schema `lm-s4c-extra-features-v1`): calendar windows (90 and 365 days) of goals for / against with counts, an exponentially weighted form (half-life 60 days), and opponent-adjusted form (goals scored minus what the opponent usually concedes, goals conceded minus what it usually scores; opponent rates shrunk toward the running league-wide mean, taken from matches available before the match was applied). Same availability rule as Sprint 4; NaN with a flag when not computable. Perturbation audit over three cut points: 0 violations.
5. **Exact-score audit** (`per_league.exact_score_metrics`, `s4c_exact_score.json`): log score of the realised scoreline, top-1 / top-5 hit rate, paired intervals against the Sprint 3 bridge model and a model-free smoothed scoreline baseline, plus the over-2.5 and BTTS base rates per competition and season.
6. **Expected goals in the prospective store** (`rollout/records.ScoreForecastStore`). The immutable `ProspectiveRecord` is NOT changed (its content hash would change and every stored record would turn into a conflict). Expected goals, the 7x7 score grid, the five most likely scorelines, P(0-0), P(1-1) and the tail mass are stored in an append-only sidecar keyed exactly like the record and carrying the record's content hash. `rollout scorecard` writes `score_scorecard.json` (log score of the realised scoreline, top-1 / top-5, expected-goals error), prospective only.
7. **Shadow families** (config `shadow.per_league`): `P_dc_sep` (separately trained per-league DC for ENG1 / ESP1 / FRA1 / ITA1) and `H_dc_hier` are written as exploratory shadow candidates with RAW probabilities (no calibrator exists for them), never user-visible. They do not enter the Sprint 5 registry and cannot be routed.

## Results (development folds 2020-21 to 2024-25, 10,712 matches; the frozen holdout is not read)
Primary metric: log loss; paired block bootstrap (ISO weeks), SUPPORTED only if the whole 95% interval of (candidate minus reference) is below 0. 340 comparisons were made; about 8 "supported" would appear by chance alone.
* **Separately trained per-league DC does not beat the pooled models.** 1X2: 1.0115 against the Sprint 3 bridge model 1.0113 (INCONCLUSIVE) and against the production Elo 0.9990 (WORSE, +0.0125). Over/under 0.6884 against production Dixon-Coles 0.6964 (SUPPORTED) but the pooled hierarchical model is better (0.6842). BTTS 0.6919 against 0.6968 (SUPPORTED).
* **Separately trained per-league boosted trees are worse than the pooled tuned trees** on all three targets (1X2 +0.0126, over/under +0.0059, BTTS +0.0059 against the same model with all leagues pooled; every interval above 0). Too little data per league; pooling wins.
* **The hierarchical model with learned variance components is the best goal model.** Against the Sprint 3 bridge model: 1X2 -0.0047, over/under -0.0073, BTTS -0.0037, exact-score log score -0.0123 (all SUPPORTED). Against the production global models: over/under -0.0122 and BTTS -0.0072 (SUPPORTED), 1X2 +0.0076 (WORSE: the production Elo is better for the result). The nested selection chooses nearly full pooling of team strength for totals and BTTS (team variance 0.0078, at the edge of the grid in 76 of 90 selections), and weak pooling for 1X2.
* **Extension features** help the tuned boosted trees a little: 1X2 -0.0024 and over/under -0.0023 (SUPPORTED), BTTS +0.0011 (INCONCLUSIVE). Against the production global models the extended trees are INCONCLUSIVE on 1X2 (-0.0013) and SUPPORTED on totals and BTTS.
* **Exact score:** top-1 hit rate 12.3% (bridge model), 12.6% (hierarchical), 11.9% (frequency baseline); log score 2.990 / 2.978 / 3.092. The goal models beat the model-free baseline; the gain over the baseline is the same for the separately trained and the pooled model.
* **"Always over 68.8%"** cannot be reproduced: in the licensed development universe the over-2.5 rate per competition and season lies between 43.9% (ESP1 2020-21) and 64.7% (ENG1 2023-24). The 138-match report behind the claim is not part of the licensed universe.
* Per-competition: separate models help totals in ENG1, FRA1, ITA1, the Europa League and the Conference League against the production Dixon-Coles, but not the result in ENG1 and FRA1 (worse than production Elo).

## Consequences and limits
* **No model is approved for users by this ADR.** The holdout is consumed (ADR 0067) and these results come from development folds on which the selection was made; the only confirmation left is prospective (shadow). The exploratory shadow families collect that evidence.
* 76 of 90 selected grid values sit on the edge of their grid: for totals and BTTS the optimum may lie at even stronger pooling than the grid allows. Stated, not extended again, to keep the run bounded.
* The pooled "ALL" rows of `P_dc_sep` mix separate models with reference fallbacks (first fold: none eligible; later folds: 1,360 to 1,826 of 1,513 to 2,233 eval rows covered); the per-competition rows are the clean comparison.
* Exact-score probabilities beyond six goals for a side are not stored in the sidecar grid (counted, not scored).
* Variance components are cross-validated, not marginal-likelihood estimates; no team-by-season random effect exists.
* The football-data.org overlay inside the shadow history stays research-only (ADR 0069), so these families can never be served before that licence is settled.

## Reproducibility
`python -m src.league_models.s4c_benchmark --as-of 2026-10-20T00:00:00Z --docs-dir docs/league_models`. Fingerprints of `s4c.yaml`, `ml_models.yaml` and `stat_models.yaml` are in `s4c_run_manifest.json`. Wall-clock limits are not used for stopping (trial counts are); the whole run was executed twice on one machine and all seven committed outputs were byte-identical (cross-machine reproduction not run).

## Rollback
Set `shadow.per_league.enabled: false` in `rollout.yaml` (no new shadow family is written); delete the `s4c_*` files, `s4c.yaml` and the modules `nested.py`, `per_league.py`, `extra_features.py`, `s4c_benchmark.py`. Stored shadow records and sidecars are append-only evidence and stay. Nothing in production imports these modules.
